# Compressed DPA4C CUDA Inference

## 1. Scope

This document describes the CUDA inference path of the compressed DPA4C descriptor as it is deployed in LAMMPS: the structure of one molecular-dynamics step on the device, where its time goes, the measured floor of each part, and the evidence behind every design decision, including the alternatives that were measured and rejected. It complements `doc/outisli/dpa4c.md`, which defines the descriptor, `doc/outisli/dpa4c_cpu.md`, which specifies the CPU kernels of the same operators, and `doc/outisli/dpa1&4c_cuda.md`, which records the per-model operators that preceded this path.

The numerical contract is fp32 model computation with TF32 disabled. A change either leaves the outputs bit for bit identical, or it changes floating-point evaluation order and is then judged against the uncompressed model in fp32 and in fp64; section 3 states the protocol. No reduced-precision arithmetic (TF32, bf16, fp16, or split-precision emulation) is used anywhere on the path.

The compressed descriptor kernels are compiled in generated shards (`source/op/pt/dpa4c/shard.cu.in`): one per angular degree for 64 and 128 channels, whose degrees take the longest, and one per width for 8, 16 and 32 channels, whose degrees share most of their code and compress into a third of the fatbinary together. A vector-only comparison of this partition gives 199 s for the longest shard against 566 s for the 128-channel width in one unit, and an operator library of 178 MB against 169 MB; one shard per degree at every width gives 216 MB, and one per degree and direction 238 MB. The widths and degrees are listed twice, in the dispatch of `graph_compress.cu` and in the shard loop of `source/op/pt/CMakeLists.txt`; on Linux the operator library links with `--no-undefined`, so an entry missing from the shard loop fails the build at the link instead of failing the load of the library at run time.

### 2.2 Systems and models

The benchmark systems are periodic diamond-carbon supercells of 512, 10,296 and 100,800 atoms (`debug/cuda_bench/systems/diamond_v1_*.data`). At the 6 Å cutoff every atom has 157.9 directed neighbor edges on average, so the three systems carry 81 thousand, 1.63 million and 15.9 million edges.

Production simulations almost always hold several elements, so every grade is also measured on a multi-element system, BCN: boron, carbon and nitrogen drawn at random, each with equal probability, for the sites of the same supercells (`systems/diamond_v1_*.bcn.data`, the `bcn` flavor of `bench_sizes.py` and `lmp_scan.py`). It keeps every neighbor list of the carbon system and differs from it in the atom types alone, so between neighbor-list rebuilds the two systems separate the cost of the types from that of the geometry.

The models are the five released v20260911 grades restored from the trained MatPES checkpoints (`model_ema.ckpt.pt`), compressed as `dp compress` does (vacuum reference folded into the energy bias, radial table stride 0.01 Å) and frozen with `debug/cuda_bench/freeze_dpa4c.py --checkpoint`:

### 2.3 Timing

The headline metric is the LAMMPS loop time per MD step of an NVT run (`debug/cuda_bench/in.lammps`, and `in_bcn.lammps` for BCN: `fix nvt`, `neighbor 1.0 bin`, `neigh_modify every 1 delay 0 check yes`), measured by `debug/cuda_bench/bench_sizes.py`. It covers the complete step: integration, neighbor check, communication, graph construction, the model and the force scatter. A single-rank run is started directly, without `mpirun`, and unbound, so the scheduler places its host process on any CPU (section 7.5). The runs of one point spread over the devices of a node, which run concurrently, and a run slows while another process on the node starts or queries the devices: on the H20 node a neighbour that creates CUDA contexts back to back lengthens a timed step of five-element Nano at 100,800 atoms by 30%, one that runs `nvidia-smi -q` by 61% and one that starts LAMMPS by 12% to 14%, so a run whose timed part overlaps the start of a run on another device takes longer; with barriers that keep every timed part clear of the starts, the range of the runs of that point falls from 26% to 36% to 3% to 4% (`devices/h20.md`, section 9.8).

The baseline is the commit the DPA4C work started from, `37f2f6c90` on upstream `dbca0b1cf`: its C++ library and LAMMPS plugin, with the five models frozen by its Python code (`models/v20260911_trained_head`). Both builds run on the public LAMMPS release, a plugin-mode build of the unmodified `patch_4Jul2026` sources (`lammps-patch_4Jul2026`), so the comparison measures the deepmd-kit side alone: the operator library, the C and C++ API, the LAMMPS plugin of `source/lmp` and the Python freeze and lower code. The OutisLi fork, which carries the native-spin integrators of section 8, also changes LAMMPS for the plain step -- the Nose-Hoover chain of `fix nvt/kk` on the device, the neighbor check on the current coordinates and a reproducible atom sort -- so the plain step is compared on the public release alone. The baseline library and plugin are built from a worktree of `37f2f6c90`; the current build is the install of the working tree, with the models of `models/v20260911_trained_rcf`.

Kernel-level attribution comes from Nsight Systems inside the LAMMPS run (`debug/cuda_bench/lmp_nsys.py`) and from Nsight Compute with `--cache-control none --clock-control none`. Section 7.1 records why the second pair of flags is mandatory for these kernels.

## 3. Correctness protocol

Every change passes the following checks before it is timed:

1. `debug/cuda_bench/bench_ops.py --compare` against stored outputs of the build a change replaces (`work_check/s4/ref_{512,10296,100800}.npz`): energy, per-atom energy, force, virial and per-atom virial of all five grades at all three sizes. A change that does not reorder a sum must report zero deviation.
1. `debug/cuda_bench/op_parity.py` for the descriptor operators alone, on random-weight presets and, with `--trained`, on the trained checkpoints, which are the only models that adopt the low-rank table of section 6.4.
1. LAMMPS per-atom energies and forces of the five trained grades at 512 and 10,296 atoms (`in_dump.lammps`, run 0, at `%.15g`), and for a change to the graph build those of the 512-atom system at the neighbor skins 0.5, 1, 2, 2.5, 4 and 18 Å (`run_fill.sh`), whose candidate lists reach both fills of section 9, compared between builds with `spin_newton/compare_text_runs.py`. Fifteen significant digits resolve every change of a fixed-point force and of any single-precision result, though not a last-bit change of a double-precision sum, which the comparison of `bench_ops.py` resolves. For spin, the dumps of `spin_newton/in_spin_point.lammps` at `%.17g` compare bit for bit.
1. For a change that reorders sums by design, `debug/cuda_bench/lowrank_deployed.py`: energy, force and virial errors of the compressed model against the uncompressed model in fp32 and in fp64, on a near-equilibrium (0.05 Å jitter) and a hot (0.3 Å jitter) 512-atom configuration. The change is accepted when its errors match those of the build it replaces, or stay below the error of the uncompressed fp32 model itself.
1. The pytest suites: `source/tests/pt_expt/descriptor/test_dpa4c_cuda.py`, `test_dpa4c_cpu.py`, `test_dpa1_cuda.py`, `source/tests/pt_expt/model/test_dpa4c_graph_lower.py`, `test_freeze_compress.py` and the serialization and charge-state suites. A fresh pair encoder mixes the mode profiles with coefficients of about 1e-4, whose errors stay below the tolerances of several comparisons, those inside a bridging window among them, so the test descriptors scale the mixing columns of its output layer by 2000: to a mean coefficient of 0.06 to 0.31 for 32 channels and more, and of 0.47 to 0.61, in part at the saturation of the `tanh` bound, for 8 and 16 channels. The largest components of the outputs that the portable comparisons then compare reach 2 to 96, and the rounding of an fp32 sum follows the magnitude of its terms rather than of its result, so the comparisons of the compressed path with the portable equations of a descriptor with radial modes scale their absolute tolerances with the largest magnitude of each output: on the co-scheduled system of 18,065 atoms the uncompressed fp32 reference alone changes between two identical runs by up to 1.3e-5 in the coordinate gradient, whose components reach 25, and the CUDA path departs from an fp64 evaluation by up to 8.1e-6, within 1e-6 of the unscaled tolerance. With the mixing of another source type or of another mode, the scans depart from the references of `test_wrong_mode_mixing_fails_the_comparisons` by 69 to 4,300 times the scaled tolerances, and with the correct mixing they stay within 0.003 to 0.05 of them. The comparison of the fused operator with its CPU reference repeats a bridged descriptor without its pair potential: the tolerance follows the largest force, and the pair forces of the short bonds of the random test clusters, up to 4.5e3 eV/Å, would leave 0.09 eV/Å to every descriptor error; without them the kernel stays within about 1e-6 of the force scale, and a wrong mixing departs by 3e-3 to 1.4e-2 of it, against a tolerance of 2e-5. The tests of the launch choices read the kernel trace, each in a fresh process, because CUPTI stops delivering kernel records late in a long process: the longest edge scan of each direction of a device-filling system must be co-scheduled and of the kind that serves the system, and systems of doubling size must take every row split from that of the smallest system down to one warp per row, each following the reference. The bond prefetch is checked by guard slots that carry the edge storage of the same system past the L2 size, with every output bitwise unchanged. The node-group width follows from the device alone, so a device runs one width per profile; the other is verified on the devices that select it.
1. The C++ and C suites of the device-resident entry points, the cases of `source/api_cc/tests/dpa4c_canonical_test_common.h` on the two fixtures of `source/tests/infer/gen_dpa4c_canonical.py`, a charge-conditioned compressed DPA4C energy model and a native-spin one on the compact canonical lower. The C++ suite runs them through `deepmd::DeepPot` and `deepmd::DeepSpin`, the C suite through `deepmd::hpp::DeepPot` and `deepmd::hpp::DeepSpin` and so through `DP_DPA4ComputeCanonicalGraphGPU` and `DP_DPA4SpinComputeCanonicalGraphGPU`. The graph that the host entry points build is evaluated through `compute_canonical_graph_gpu` eagerly, captured and replayed, and every evaluation must equal the host result exactly, magnetic force included. The outputs are reset to NaN before every call, so a replay that writes nothing fails, and a capture that falls back to eager evaluation fails its case through the warning it prints. The cases cover the current stream; a caller's stream that a spin kernel holds back before every call, so that an evaluation enqueued on any other stream reads back as NaN; calls without the source CSR and without the virial; inputs rewritten in place between replays, a uniform strain of the geometry, swapped atom types and, for the spin model, other moments; outputs at other addresses, which release the captured graph and are captured in turn; a graph with halo nodes, which runs eagerly and whose folded forces match the host result to the summation order of its run; a replay and an eager evaluation, both without a caller's stream, while another thread holds a capture of its own open, which must stay valid; and a runtime charge state. The host results themselves are checked against the Python inference of the archives. Each of six faults fails the suite: a replay that launches nothing, an entry that ignores the caller's stream, a capture that always fails, a spin entry that leaves the magnetic force unwritten, one that reads a private copy of the moments, and the wait for the whole device at the end of a call without a caller's stream.
1. The LAMMPS regression of the Kokkos pair styles, `source/lmp/tests/test_dpa4spin_kokkos_magnetic_force.py`, which `source/install/test_lammps_kokkos.sh` runs against a Kokkos build of LAMMPS and the plugin of the tree; it is the only test of a Kokkos pair style. It evaluates `dpa4spin/kk` on six atoms of NiO with one and with two ranks against the host pair style `dpa4spin`, for every magnetic-force convention and under `hybrid/overlay/kk`. The communication settings of its Kokkos runs (`comm host`, `gpu/aware off`) put the two-rank runs on the host path of the pair reverse communication and so on the device-packed fold of section 7.5: with the six values of force and magnetic force in every run, and with the nine of the centroid virial in the runs after a `compute centroid/stress/atom` is defined, which switches the graph build and the operator to their source-attributed path while the earlier runs keep the path without it. A plugin without the nine-value fold fails all six cases. `deepmd/kk` has no Kokkos regression; its folds of three and nine values were compared with the host path of LAMMPS by hand (section 7.5).

For a fixed index width and device, run-to-run reproducibility is part of the contract: every reduction in the path has a fixed order, the force is accumulated in 64-bit fixed point (section 6.3), and the row order of the canonical graph follows from the bond lengths and atom tags alone (section 6.1), so the model returns the same bits for the same configuration whatever the local order of the atoms. A domain decomposition enters only through the node count of each rank's run, which selects the row split, the co-scheduled blocks and the node-group width (section 9); a fixed-point total converts to fp64 exactly, so the reverse communication of ghost forces adds without rounding, and two MPI ranks reproduce the per-atom energies, forces and pressure of one rank bit for bit at 10,296 atoms (Neo and Plus).

A whole MD run is reproducible only if LAMMPS is as well. The Kokkos atom sort of the public LAMMPS leaves the atoms of one sort bin in the order of atomic increments, so identical runs hold their atoms in different local orders (178 and 189 of 512 positions differed after the setup sort), the temperature sums in a different order and differs in its last bits, and a thermostat that scales the velocities by it makes identical NVT runs diverge. The fork sorts the atoms within their bins by position (commit `ab3f4ce`); with it, four identical 200-step NVT runs and four identical 4,000-step Newtonian spin runs agree bit for bit in their final states and in their thermo output at `%.17g`, and the step time is unchanged.

### 4.3 Automatic source-index width

The compact deployment graph carries source-node indices and explicit `float32[S,3]` edge vectors. A node domain of at most 2^31 entries uses four-byte source words; a larger domain uses eight-byte words. Edge counts, storage capacities, destination/source row pointers and source-grouped edge positions always use int64. More than 2^32 edges alone therefore does not require wider node indices. LAMMPS's per-rank local-plus-ghost limit permits four-byte source words even in a simulation with many more global atoms.

One frozen archive accepts both widths through a `uint8[S,W]` source tensor, whose dynamic final extent is four or eight bytes. The CUDA operator restores a typed view without copying or transferring index data. Ordinary models retain eight inputs and native-spin models nine. The C and C++ device entry points carry the source pointer and `DP_CanonicalIndexWidth` explicitly; their replay key includes the width, including a change at the same buffer address. Artifacts with the fixed-width tensor contract must be re-frozen, and the C library, backend and LAMMPS plugins must be rebuilt together.

The descriptor uses the vector kernels and their device-derived dispatch: occupancy, SM count, L2 capacity, cluster support and available kernel images determine the eligible mapping. No geometry calibration, second replay pool, GPU-model whitelist or public tuning switch is present. The position pack remains internal to the LAMMPS vector producer. Normal floating-point contraction follows the numerical contract of section 3.

The indexed implementation and its measurements are recorded as a rejected experiment in section 12.3. Its full two-device matrices are historical evidence, not validation of this eight-/nine-input interface.

The final vector interface passes 113 related model, graph, metadata and ZBL tests on both RTX PRO 6000 and H20, plus four compact-DPA1 tests; one DPA1 case requires the separate high-angular-degree build option and is skipped. ## 5. Structure of one step

One NVT step of the deployed path runs, in stream order:

1. LAMMPS: the first half of the Nose-Hoover chain and the velocity update, the position update, the neighbor-distance check and forward communication.
1. `pair_style deepmd/kk`: the compact canonical graph build (`source/lmp/compact_canonical_graph_kokkos.h`), which writes, for every local atom, its in-cutoff neighbors sorted by squared distance and source tag, as uint32 source indices, fp32 bond vectors and int64 destination row pointers.
1. The model, replayed from a captured CUDA graph (`ptexpt::GraphReplay` in `source/api_cc/src/commonPTExpt.h`), whose body is one call of the fused operator `deepmd::dpa4c_canonical_compress_energy_force` (`source/op/pt/dpa4c/graph_compress.cu`):
   1. a prologue kernel: ownership mask, energy seed, node types, cleared force words;
   1. the forward edge scan (degree-wise moments of every row) and the forward readout (invariant descriptor);
   1. the fused fitting network: energy and descriptor cotangent in one kernel (`source/op/pt/graph_fitting.cu`);
   1. the node backward (readout VJP into moment cotangents) and the edge backward (per-edge radial and angular VJP, force assembly in 64-bit fixed point, node virial);
   1. an epilogue kernel: fixed-point to fp64 conversion, frame energy and virial, per-atom energy.
1. LAMMPS: force scatter, the second half of the integration and of the thermostat chain.

The model graph holds 11 to 16 kernels at 100,800 atoms (11 for Nano to Air, 16 for Plus, whose fitting runs layer by layer) and 12 at 512 atoms; at 512 atoms its kernels take about 58 µs per step for Nano and span 60 µs, in a median step of 165 µs under the profiler on the public LAMMPS (`lmp_nsys.py`, section 7.3). Its last step writes the fp64 results into the outputs of the caller (`ptexpt::copy_results`): up to 8,192 nodes in one multi-tensor copy, a single kernel, and beyond that one copy per tensor, since the multi-tensor copy serves each chunk of 65,536 elements with one block and so leaves most of the device idle on the force of a large system; inside the replay, the copy takes 18 µs in one kernel and 8 µs in three copies at 100,800 nodes.

## 6. Design elements

### 6.1 Graph construction

The canonical graph is rebuilt every step, because the bond vectors change every step and the canonical row order follows the distances. The builder fills all rows in one pass: each block of eight centers stages the neighbor-list entries of its centers into shared memory, keys the candidates by fixed-point squared distance, drops those outside the cutoff, sorts each row in a warp, and derives its output offset by a decoupled look-back over the sums published by earlier blocks. Positions are packed as fixed-point integers once per step, so the displacements are exact integers, and the squared lengths that key the row order follow from them in single precision by a fixed sequence of operations: the order is a function of the fixed-point positions and the atom tags alone. When the step needs no source CSR, as a step without a per-atom virial does, and the candidate storage takes at most 1/64 of the device memory that CUDA reports, edges are written into storage sized by the candidate count, which removes the per-step edge-count readback and its synchronization. The readback holds the host until the fill ends, so the device idles while the host launches the model: under Nsight Systems the device waits 25 to 34 µs per step between the end of the fill and the start of the model with the readback, and 4 µs without it (Nano at 49,248 and 100,800 atoms, Mini and Neo at 100,800), which saves 0.7% to 3% of their steps; plain runs, whose step times spread by about 2%, do not resolve a difference this small. The bound, 1.59 GB here, covers about 360,000 atoms of the diamond systems at a neighbor skin of 1 Å, and costs the storage of the candidates outside the cutoff, 0.3 GB at 150,000 atoms.

Five elements shorten the dependent steps of a block. The staging is a set of asynchronous copies (`cp.async`, compute capability 8.0 and later) that fetch the whole 128-byte line of each entry into L2 (`.L2::128B`): a line holds one candidate slot of 32 consecutive centers, which four consecutive blocks stage at about the same time, so each line is read from DRAM once and in one piece. The loads of the model node, the candidate count and the type of every center are requested together, the copies follow once the counts arrive, and the node types are written before the copies are awaited. Lists of up to twelve candidates per lane take a kernel of their own, which keeps four blocks, 32 warps, resident per multiprocessor at 64 registers. Each warp sorts its survivors in the lane-major layout, element `lane * K + k` in register `k`, in which only the 15 stages of the bitonic network whose partners lie in other lanes shuffle, against 30 of the 36 stages of a 256-key sort in the register-major layout; the sorted source atoms then pass through the warp's row of shared memory back to the register-major order, so that the stores of a warp still cover consecutive edges. The first warp of a block to finish its sort runs the look-back, by which time the blocks before it have had the time of that sort to publish their sums; the total and the inclusive prefix of a block are then stored by different warps and may reach its status word in either order, which leaves every offset exact, since a later block that reads the total looks further back. The row order follows from the keys alone, so these elements leave every output bit for bit as it is without them. With vector geometry at 100,800 atoms (Nano, `lmp_nsys.py`) the fill takes 0.31 ms against 0.47 ms without them: the twelve-candidate kernel gives 0.03 ms, the asynchronous staging 0.02 ms, the lane-major sort with the look-back after it 0.07 ms, the look-back by the first warp to finish 0.004 ms, and the line fetch into L2 0.03 ms. It moves its 385 MB at 1.25 TB/s, 70% of the DRAM bandwidth, and remains the largest non-model kernel of the step; at 512 atoms it takes 10 µs.

The C-compatible `DP_CanonicalIndexWidth` declares the source word width, while edge positions and CSR arrays remain int64. The graph contains only model-node indices and `float32[S,3]` bond vectors. The producer packs each atom's fixed-point coordinates and model-node index into one internal 16-byte record, then emits the same explicit vectors consumed by the model. The counted fill retains its double-precision subtraction before conversion to float32. The double-precision model coordinates reserved for long-range evaluation remain a separate input.

The single-pass look-back represents the nonnegative int64 edge axis in a 64-bit status word: zero is unpublished, a block total is stored plus one, and bit 63 marks an inclusive prefix. The position-pack kernel resets every status word and the ticket counter before each fill attempt, including a retry after capacity growth. Destination offsets, source counts, cursors and sorted source-edge positions retain this width; there is no 32-bit edge-count saturation.

Source indices and edge vectors grow together and share one used-prefix watermark. A fill clears the rows used by its predecessor beyond its own physical end, so the retained capacity contains zero guards outside the destination CSR.

LAMMPS' neighbor-rebuild flag caches the candidate counts and atom-to-model maps, but does not make a compact graph immutable between rebuilds. Atoms cross the model cutoff and distances change the row order while the skin neighbor list stays valid. Reusing the previous compact source list would also lose the identity of periodic images after node folding. The vector fill therefore recomputes membership and order at every step.

### 6.2 Replay of the model graph

The model evaluation of a graph without halo nodes keeps its tensor extents from step to step, so it is captured once into a CUDA graph and replayed, keyed by the addresses and extents of its inputs and the canonical index width. The canonical graph builder writes into storage whose address only changes when the capacity grows, and the LAMMPS output buffers are sized by the model node set rather than by every atom, so the replay serves every step between neighbor-list rebuilds. A replay costs one `cudaGraphLaunch` instead of 11 to 16 kernel launches. The tensors over the caller's buffers are built inside the evaluation, which a replay skips: wrapping a device buffer in a tensor queries the driver for the device that holds it, and a replayed call takes 8.6 µs of host time with the wrapping inside the evaluation against 24 µs (energy) and 30 µs (spin) around it. The step time of LAMMPS stays the same within 0.6% either way at 512 and 10,296 atoms, because the host prepares the replay while the device still runs the graph build.

The captured graph reads the model constants where they reside. Applying a charge state at run time rewrites them, so it releases the replay first, and the next call evaluates eagerly before the one after it captures again. `debug/cuda_bench/charge_replay.py` checks this through the C API: three calls under the charge state frozen into the model, `set_charge_spin`, three more calls, each compared bit for bit with a fresh instance evaluated eagerly under the same state. The device-resident entry points of the C API are `DP_DPA4ComputeCanonicalGraphGPU` and `DP_DPA4SpinComputeCanonicalGraphGPU` (API version 31), which take the source pointer, its word width, explicit edge vectors, the virial of the graph and the caller's stream. The C library, C++ backend and plugin consumers use the same source-width contract and must be built together. The first also takes the positions of the nodes and the MPI communicator of a system on several ranks, which only a model with a long-range module reads (`DP_DeepPotHasLongRange`); such a model evaluates a complete non-periodic system, on several ranks on every rank at every step, a rank without nodes included, and its evaluation with a communicator is never captured, since it may communicate between its kernels, while the address of its positions joins the key of the replay otherwise. In this tree no model reports a long-range module; the interfaces are reserved for the long-range work. They take the place of `DP_DeepPotComputeCanonicalGraphGPU` and `DP_DeepSpinComputeCanonicalGraphGPU` of API versions 28 to 30, whose argument lists differ, under other names, so that a binary built against an earlier version fails to load instead of calling a function of another argument list; the C++ wrappers carry the one signature as well. A capture that fails at any stage, including the instantiation of the graph, ends the capture so that the stream leaves capture mode, discards the incomplete graph, and turns capturing off for that model: later calls evaluate eagerly, since a retry would meet the same failure while holding another private memory pool, and an error of the evaluation itself recurs in the eager call, which reports it.

An instance runs its model one call at a time: the model runs on the single-threaded AOTInductor runner, which records no completion event per run and so lets a run be enqueued on a caller's stream and captured, and a per-instance mutex serializes the runs of the model, the device-resident entry points, whose replay keeps its graph across calls, and the replacement of the constants. Different models evaluate concurrently from different threads of one process. A wait for the whole device is invalid while any stream of the device captures, and it invalidates the capture, so the operations of the backend that wait for the whole device -- a capture, the release of a graph and a charge-state update -- hold one process-wide lock and take turns, and a call without a caller's stream waits for the current stream of its thread, on which it enqueues all of its work, rather than for the device. CUDA work outside DeePMD-kit that waits for the whole device while a capture is under way still invalidates that capture, and the model then evaluates without replay; PyTorch's own graph capture has the same restriction. Applying a charge state waits for the device on both sides of the rebuild of the frozen tables: before it, because an evaluation still in flight on a caller's stream reads the constants the rebuild overwrites, and after it, because the next evaluation may run on another stream. The rebuilt tables are written into the active constants in place (`load_constants` with `use_inactive=false`), which keeps their addresses, marks the constants for folding at the next call and holds no second copy of them. A step that requests the per-atom virial evaluates more outputs than the steps around it and so changes the key: that step and the next one evaluate eagerly, and the replay resumes one step later. One replay retains the current input and output addresses, extents and source-index width. Reallocating a buffer or changing its width invalidates that replay; changing the contents in place preserves it. The backend interface of `DeepPotBackend` and `DeepSpinBackend` carries the virial of the graph and the stream, so the backend plugins export their DeepPot and DeepSpin entry points as version 2 (`deepmd_create_deeppot_backend_v2`), and a plugin built against the interface of version 1 is refused when it is loaded instead of being called through a table of another layout.

### 6.3 Fused energy-force operator

The operator takes the compact graph and returns the frame energy, the per-atom energy, the force, the frame virial, the per-atom virial, for a spin model the magnetic force, and the ownership mask. The work around the descriptor -- the ownership mask, the frame offsets, the segment sums and the dtype conversions -- runs in one prologue and one epilogue kernel inside it rather than as separate kernels of the traced program.

The edge backward assembles the force directly. Each edge adds its gradient to the destination row in registers and subtracts it from the source atom with 64-bit integer atomics on a fixed-point representation (2^-32 eV/Å resolution), four words per atom so the three components of an edge share one 32-byte sector. Integer addition is associative, so the force is independent of the scheduling order and the result is reproducible; no per-edge gradient is stored and no source-major pass is needed. The per-atom virial is attributed to source atoms through the source CSR when it is requested. A caller that declines it passes two-entry placeholders in place of the CSR, which LAMMPS then does not build, and the operator returns the per-atom virial as zeros, a fill of 36 bytes per atom, without storing any edge cotangent. The Kokkos builder sorts every row of the source CSR by edge position, so the per-atom virial, like the force, is the same from run to run.

A fixed-point word cannot hold a non-finite value, so the epilogue checks the frame totals instead: a non-finite descriptor reaches the energy and a non-finite edge gradient the virial, and a frame whose energy or virial is not finite reports every one of its forces, and for a spin model every magnetic force, as NaN. The words of an atom hold totals below 2^31 eV/Å; a single contribution of 2^30 eV/Å or more, which the ZBL pair term reaches only below about 7·10^-4 Å for carbon and 0.01 Å for the heaviest elements, sets the fourth word of the atom it lands on, and the epilogue then reports the energy of that atom, and with it its frame, as NaN. The magnetic force of a spin model is assembled in the same fixed point, from the magnetic contribution of every edge onto its source atom, and a magnetic contribution of 2^30 or more sets the same word. The check covers each contribution: a total that leaves the range through three or more smaller contributions on one atom, which takes three neighbours within 0.001 Å (carbon) to 0.01 Å (uranium) of it under the unscreened Coulomb repulsion, is not detected; making it exact would add a fourth atomic to every source scatter of the edge backward, the operation that bounds that kernel. The check is one compare per scattered component and costs nothing measurable (`bench_ops.py`, three rotated repeats at 10,296 and 100,800 atoms: −2.1% to +0.9%), while a per-row maximum of the contributions, which holds one more register through the scan, costs up to 3%. The conditional atomic does change the code the compiler generates around it: in the co-scheduled backward scan of Air it fuses one multiply and add less (3,107 FFMA instructions against 3,110 without the check), so Air's forces at 10,296 and 100,800 atoms differ from those of the build without the check by up to 1.2·10⁻⁷ relative, the same from run to run. A per-row predicate, which keeps the branch out of the scan, changes them as much, so no form of the check preserves the rounding of the build without it. The change is eight times smaller than the error of the compression against fp64 (1.0·10⁻⁶ relative rms), which `lowrank_deployed.py` reproduces to three digits with and without the check. A collision cascade of a ZBL-bridged model stays below the limit: a 100 keV carbon recoil turns at about 0.005 Å, where the pair force is about 2·10^7 eV/Å.

### 6.4 Edge scans

Both edge scans assign one destination row to one warp, split into edge groups of 2 to 16 lanes; every lane of a group owns a fixed subset of the channels. Several mechanisms adapt this mapping to the system:

- Split rows. A system with fewer rows than the device holds resident warps shares each row among 2, 4 or 8 warps of one block; the warps scan disjoint edges and merge their accumulators in warp order. The split is the largest power of two up to 8 whose warps all fit in one wave of the warps that the same scan keeps resident in its blocks of independent rows, a function of the node count of the system and of the occupancy the device reports for that kernel, so the summation order is fixed for a given system, kernel and device, and a system split into runs by the node tile takes the split of the whole system in every run. Eight warps per row, the largest split, applies below roughly 560 rows to a kernel that keeps 24 blocks resident per multiprocessor on this device, and below proportionally fewer rows to a kernel of lower occupancy, such as the spin and bridged edge backward of Nano; it shortens the 512-atom step of Mini, Neo and Air by 2% to 4% and leaves Nano unchanged.
- Lockstep rows. The wide profiles (128 channels, and the degree-three backward of 64 channels) run 24 rows per block, and their native-spin counterparts 20, whose 640 threads keep 96 registers for the spin payload (section 7.4), in windows of 4 Å of bond length, so that the rows of a block read the same region of the radial table at the same time. A block walks the windows from the lowest that holds the first bond of one of its rows to the highest that holds the last bond of one of them. The positions a row visits in each window, and with them the order of its sums, follow from the row alone, whichever rows share its block, when the row is sorted by bond length, as LAMMPS sorts it, and for any row while the radial table spans at most two windows, below a cutoff of 8 Å, which covers the presets at 6 Å; only an unsorted row, such as the Python graph builders keep, at a cutoff of 8 Å or more can be split at the windows of the rows that share its block.
- Folded single-type scans. When every node of the system has one type, a spin-free scan of 32 channels or more and at most four mode profiles moves the PairFiLM scale, shift and mode mixing from every edge to the node: it accumulates the bare radial features and applies the pair coefficients once per row. Only a system of one element takes it; a system of several elements, the usual case in production, scans its edges slotted or pairwise (next item; section 7.2 compares the scans on the carbon and BCN systems, and section 7.4 records the build without the folded path).
- Slotted scans. The rows of a system of at most eight node types hold edges of at most eight ordered pairs, and every moment is linear in the pair coefficients of each edge, so the scans of such a system accumulate the features that a pair coefficient multiplies for the edges of each source type apart, in the slot of that type, and apply the coefficients of the pair of each slot once per row: to the reduced sums in the forward and to the node cotangents in the backward. Two kinds of feature are slotted. The mode mixing adds `Σ_r M[p, c, r] q_r` to the amplitude of channel `c` of an edge of the ordered pair `p`: a lane holds the nine harmonic moments of one mode of one slot per tile, so the four slots of four modes take 144 accumulators per edge group, and eight slots 288, and the slots enter both the degree-zero and the angular moments, as the extra features of a single-type scan do. For a descriptor that carries the low-rank table (below), the channels from `C1` on feed degree zero alone, and their FiLM amplitude `γ_{p,c} Σ_k V_{kc} b_k + β_{p,c}` is linear in the 32 basis functions `b_k` and in the constant that the shift multiplies: a lane accumulates the bare sums of its basis columns for every slot, and lane `s` of an edge group the envelope sum of slot `s`; at the end of the row each edge group expands the sums of its share of the slots into the channels and applies the FiLM of the pair of each slot, and the backward forms the cotangents of the basis functions and of the shift of each slot once per row and reads those of the slot of every edge from shared memory. The channels below `C1`, which feed the angular degrees, keep their FiLM on the edges. The native-spin profiles of Air and Plus slot their mode profiles as well, in systems of one to eight types, since they have no single-type scan; the spin families weight the moment of every neighbour and stay on the edges, and the spin backward keeps the slot cotangents in shared memory. The scan of the node types in the prologue publishes a bitmap of the types of the system, from which the device knows the number of types: every edge-scan launch of a profile that slots starts the kernel of the other scans and the slotted kernels, the number of types selects the one that scans the rows, and every block of the others ends at once, through a predicated PTX exit on the bitmap alone, which leaves the generated code of the pairwise and single-type scans as it is (section 7.4). A slot adds its sums to the state of every lane and its predicated or zero-weighted updates to every edge whatever the system, so a system of up to four types takes kernels of four slots and one of five to eight types kernels of eight, both launched with the others; Neo, which slots its basis functions alone, runs the forward of eight slots and the pairwise edge backward for five to eight types (section 7.4). The slotted kernels serve every row split, and below 128 channels they run blocks of four independent rows, which retire in a quarter of the time of one-row blocks when they do not serve the system; at 128 channels the forward of four slots keeps the co-scheduled blocks of `Lockstep`, and the backward and the forward of eight slots run co-scheduled blocks of sixteen rows; the spin profile of 128 channels runs co-scheduled blocks of twenty rows in both directions but for the forward of eight slots, which runs blocks of sixteen rows, and the spin backward of Air, which co-schedules like its pairwise backward, blocks of sixteen. On BCN at 100,800 atoms the forward scan and edge backward take 0.95 and 1.76 ms for Neo against 1.20 and 2.33 ms pairwise, 1.67 and 3.06 ms for Air against 3.50 and 4.01 ms, and 2.42 and 3.52 ms for Plus against 5.77 and 8.87 ms (Nsight Systems, public LAMMPS, between neighbor-list rebuilds), and the native-spin step of Air and Plus is 27% to 37% shorter at 10,296 and 100,800 atoms on carbon and on BCN (section 8.1). On five elements, on the frozen geometry of section 10, the forward of eight slots takes 1.10 ms for Neo against 1.20 ms pairwise, and the forward scan and edge backward of eight slots 2.23 and 3.68 ms for Air against 3.57 and 4.32 ms and 3.04 and 4.50 ms for Plus against 6.35 and 9.03 ms at 100,800 atoms, which shortens the step by 2%, 19% and 37% there and by 3%, 24% and 40% at 10,296 atoms, and the native-spin step of Air and Plus by 26% to 33%. A system of more than eight types, and every system of Nano and Mini, which carry neither radial modes nor a low-rank table, scan pairwise.
- Low-rank radial table. The scalar-only channels from `C1` on feed degree zero alone, so their moments are linear in the channel values. A trained radial map spans far fewer independent functions than it has channels: on the released checkpoints, the leading 32 singular vectors of the scalar-only channels, values and slopes weighted alike, reproduce every channel at the knots to between 2e-11 and 4e-10 of its magnitude, and its radial derivative to between 9e-11 and 3e-9. At compression, `build_low_rank_table` (`deepmd/pt_expt/kernels/dpa4c/graph_compress.py`) computes that basis from the fp64 knot data of a spin-free model of 64 or 128 channels and at most four mode profiles, the models whose single-type scans fold, adopts it only if the knots give more samples than the basis has functions and every channel and its radial derivative are reproduced to 1e-8 of their magnitudes, and tabulates the degree-one channels, the 32 basis functions and the modes in the layout and on the knots of the radial table. A single-type scan of a model that carries the table reads 40 or 48 columns instead of 64 or 128 and expands the basis sums into the channel sums once per node; the backward projects the channel cotangents onto the basis once per node. Random-weight models are of high rank, fail the check and keep the direct table, so their outputs are unchanged. The low-rank scan shortens the step of Neo by 2% to 11%, of Air by 2% to 5% and of Plus by 7% to 10%, with the accuracy of section 4.2.
- Bond-vector prefetch. The bond vector heads the dependency chain of every edge: its length selects the table interval that every channel reads. When the bond vectors of the system exceed the L2 cache, they stream from DRAM and the first use of every line stalls; each warp then requests the vectors of its row into L2 when the row starts, one `prefetch.global.L2` per 128-byte line, the lanes of the warp taking one line each. The gate compares the bond vectors of the edge storage, 12 bytes per slot, with the L2 size the device reports; storage sized by the candidates of LAMMPS (section 6.1), 1.8 times the edges of the diamond systems, opens it at about 40,000 atoms instead of 70,000, and at 49,248 atoms the prefetch neither shortens nor lengthens the step (Nano, 0.851 ms with it against 0.854 ms without it, medians of three alternating plain runs on the public LAMMPS, `results/ab_prefetch_nano_49248.txt`). At 100,800 atoms the prefetch shortens the Neo forward scan by 17% and its backward by 8%, and the whole step of Neo and Mini by 4% to 7%; it is a pure hint and leaves the outputs bit for bit unchanged. The slotted scans request the source indices of the row as well, whose types select the slot of every edge: at 100,800 atoms on BCN this shortens the step of Neo by 3.7%, of Air by 3.6% and of Plus by 2.4%, and at 10,296 atoms by 0.6% to 1.8%.
- Row order of the forward scan. The LAMMPS graph builder writes the rows in ascending order, so at 100,800 atoms, whose 254 MB of edges exceed the 128 MB of L2, the rows it writes last are still in L2 when the forward scan starts, as long as one run covers the system (131,072 nodes, section 9). The blocks of one row of the profiles up to degree two take their rows from the last to the first and read those rows from L2: at 100,800 atoms the forward scan of Nano takes 0.290 ms against 0.314 ms, that of Mini 2.9% and that of Neo 1.8% less time (medians of three alternating Nsight Systems runs, `results/ab_forward_order_100800.txt`), the edge backward of Nano, which starts on the rows the forward reads last, 1.0% less, and the Newtonian spin step of Nano 1.3% less. The lockstep blocks of the wide profiles, and the blocks of the profiles of degree three and four, take their rows in ascending order (section 7.4). A block computes its rows alone, so the order of the blocks changes no sum, and the reversed kernels return every output bit for bit as in ascending order.
- Window bound of bridged models. A model bridged to the ZBL potential (`doc/outisli/dpa4c.md`) replaces, for an edge shorter than `f_outer` times the contact length of its pair, the length the table reads by a clamped one and the envelope by a switched one. The contact length is the sum of the covalent radii of the two atoms, so the test of an edge needs the radius of its source type. Every row first compares its bonds with one bound, `f_outer` times the sum of the radius of its center and the largest radius among the node types of the system, which the node-type scan of the prologue publishes, and resolves the window only below it. Rounding is monotone, so no bond at or above the bound lies inside its window, and the outputs stay bit for bit unchanged. At 10,296 atoms the bridged models then take 0.2% (Nano), 4.0% (Mini), 2.6% (Neo), 5.2% (Air) and 8.8% (Plus) longer per step than the same models without the bridge, against 1.5%, 5.6%, 4.5%, 5.8% and 11.0% without the bound; at 512 atoms the bridged Neo runs as fast as the plain one (−0.2%, against +2.8% without the bound). The remaining cost of Plus is the register state of its bridged edge backward, which keeps 144 bytes of stack against 96 for the plain kernel.

### 6.5 Readout and node backward

Both node kernels stage the moments of a node into shared memory before the readout arithmetic. The staging copy issues all of a lane's loads before its first store (`stage_shared` in `graph_compress.cuh`), so the load latencies overlap instead of adding up along the copy; with a strided copy, which stores each value after its load, that copy is the largest single stall of both kernels on Plus at 100,800 atoms (19% and 22% of their stall samples), and staging this way shortens the Plus step by 1% to 4% and the Air step at 100,800 atoms by 2%. The node backward of degree three and four requests the descriptor-cotangent lines of its node into L1 before it starts, because its shared-memory footprint leaves few warps per SM to hide the first touch of each line.

### 6.6 Fused fitting network

The fitting network runs as one kernel per node panel that carries the panel through all hidden layers, the energy head and the input-gradient backward. Activations stay feature-major in shared memory, the weights stream through a ring of `cp.async` slices that spans all GEMMs of the pass, and every GEMM sums its contraction in ascending order with one fused multiply-add per term, and every kernel adds the identity residual with explicitly rounded additions, so that no compiler can contract it with the activation product in one kernel and not in another; the energy and the descriptor cotangent are therefore bitwise those of the per-layer kernels whatever the code generation. `test_fused_network_matches_the_layer_kernels_bitwise` (`source/tests/pt_expt/descriptor/test_dpa1_cuda.py`) checks this for every compiled width and activation on runs sized by the multiprocessor count, which reach the cluster, latency and throughput shapes and the per-layer fallback, untiled and tiled, for an input narrower than the width, an input wider than it, whose layer-0 backward contracts in several passes, and a single square layer without biases.

A small system has too few panels to occupy the device, so its panel is split over a thread-block cluster of K CTAs, each of which computes and streams 1/K of the columns of every GEMM; the column slices are exchanged through L2, which on this device delivers 16 to 21 B/clk per SM against at most 4 B/clk for distributed shared memory. K is the largest divisor of `width / 32` (up to 12 with the non-portable attribute) for which `cudaOccupancyMaxActiveClusters` keeps every panel resident, and the cluster shapes are only used by a kernel image compiled for compute capability 9.0 or later, as reported by `cudaFuncGetAttributes`. At 512 nodes the cluster kernel runs the fitting in 0.61 to 0.64 of the per-layer time for widths 96 to 256 and 0.91 for width 384.

A large run of a width of 96 takes 64-node panels of twelve warps, two CTAs per SM, once it fills two waves of them or passes in fewer waves than the 32-node panels of three CTAs per SM, and the 32-node panels otherwise, each at the CTAs per SM that the device keeps resident. The larger panels read every weight slice for twice the nodes and serve 100,800 nodes in 0.28 ms against 0.30 ms, and 131,072 nodes, the default node tile (section 9), in 11% less time (`bench_fitting.py`, which times `graph_fitting_energy_gradient` on the network of Nano); below two waves a wave fewer outweighs the cost of the last, partly filled one, and on the 92 SMs of an L20 one wave of 64-node panels takes 0.064 ms at 10,296 nodes against 0.078 ms for two waves of 32-node panels. The other widths keep their shapes, whose alternatives come within 1.2% of them (section 7.4).

The fitting rate at 100,800 atoms in the LAMMPS step is the FP32 SIMT throughput the tiles reach on these skinny shapes, 38 TFLOP/s at a width of 96 (Nano) and 52 TFLOP/s at a width of 256 (Neo); section 7.4 explains why tensor cores are not used.

The route of each run, the per-layer kernels or one of the fused shapes compiled for its width, is measured when the model is frozen, on the device it is frozen for, because the time of a route follows the waves of its grid and the fill of the last one, and the break-even fill between the routes is a property of the device: on an L20 the rules leave Neo at 6,160 and 8,000 atoms on fused panels whose last wave is half full, 11.2% and 8.7% slower per LAMMPS step than the per-layer kernels. The measurement divides the run sizes into bands within which no compared route changes its number of waves, times the whole fused operator at both ends of every band, and bisects a band whose ends disagree for the size at which the winner changes. Each sample times every route on a periodic box of exactly that many atoms of three node types at the density of diamond, its rows in bond-length order, as replays of captured CUDA graphs, which is how LAMMPS evaluates the operator, with the replays of the routes alternating within each capture; a route other than that of the rules wins a sample only where its gain over the rules, the median over the captures of a sample of their per-capture ratios, exceeds both 1% and the half-range of those ratios, on three captures at least, since the medians of different captures of one route scatter by up to 3% at large runs.

Alternating replays run the routes at one clock, while a board at its power limit clocks a route by the power that the route itself draws. On an L20 at its limit of 350 W the per-layer kernels of the native-spin Neo at 100,800 atoms run at 1,830 MHz where they run alone and the fused shape of the rules at 2,295 MHz (`route_probe.py`, with the clocks sampled by `nvidia-smi` every 200 ms), so the per-layer kernels, 4% to 5% faster in alternating replays, are 9% slower on their own; a table measured on alternating replays alone, which sent the native-spin runs of Neo and Air from about 12,000 nodes up to them, lengthened the LAMMPS step of the native-spin Neo by 10% at 100,800 atoms and by 3% at 23,040. The measurement therefore also times trains of one route at a time, each after at least 500 ms of device time of its own replays, since the clock of the L20 follows a change of route within 0.4 s. Alternating replays and trains bracket a deployment, whose LAMMPS step leaves the device idle between the evaluations of the operator at small and medium systems and keeps it busy at large ones: on a V100 at its limit of 300 W, where the step of Mini at 10,296 atoms leaves the device idle 12% to 14% of the time, the per-layer kernels of Mini at 10,296 nodes are 2.6% faster in alternating replays and 0.9% faster on trains, a gain that rises to 2.0% where a fifth of the device time is idle, while the route of the rules takes 1.9% to 2.8% longer per LAMMPS step on carbon, BCN and five elements.

Under the power limit the node types of the system decide as well. At one clock the fitting network takes the same time whatever the types, but the descriptor runs the single-type scans for one type and the slotted or pairwise scans for several (section 6.4), and so sets the power and the length of the step around the fitting network. On the L20 the trains of the per-layer kernels of the native-spin Air at 129,536 and 131,072 nodes take 19.7 to 20.2 ms whatever the types, while those of the rules take 18.3 to 18.7 ms with every node of one type, 19.2 to 19.5 ms with two types and 20.0 to 20.2 ms with three (`debug/cuda_bench/route_reliability.py`, eight repeats of the check per composition, `devices/l20.md` section 11.6.2): the per-layer kernels lose 3% to 7% with every node hydrogen, carbon or iron, 2% to 4% with hydrogen and carbon, and at most about 1% with three types. The trained Air at 12,320 nodes shows the converse, a gain of 1.1% on trains with every node carbon and a loss of 1.4% with three types, so neither composition bounds the other. Below the power limit, at 8,000 nodes, the gain is the same for every composition, 9.2% to 9.8%.

A route other than that of the rules is therefore a candidate where it wins a sample of alternating replays, and it takes the run size only where it also holds on trains of both compositions, first with every node of the first type and then with the node types of the sample: each composition takes two captures, which time the routes in opposite orders so that a drift of the clocks cancels, and the median gain of each composition has to be at least -1%: two routes within the tie of 1% count as equally fast on trains, as they do in alternating replays, while the losses that the trains are timed to catch, those of a board at its power limit, reach 3% to 12% on the L20, well beyond the tie. The check of a candidate stops at the first composition on which it loses by more, the candidates are checked in the order of their gains, and the first that holds takes the run size; a model of one node type trains its one composition once. A threshold of a positive median gain refuses real gains: on the V100 the alternating replays select the per-layer kernels of Mini at 10,241 nodes, the low end of the band of 10,241 to 15,360, in 40 of 40 samples (median gain 1.9%), while on the trains of the node types of the sample their median gain is 0.25% (-0.76% to +1.06% over the 40), so that such a threshold refused them in 9 of the 40 and gave the whole band to the rules in four of six measurements of the model, which lengthens the LAMMPS step at 10,296 atoms by 2.8% on carbon, 2.1% on BCN and 1.9% on five elements. With the tie the measurement departs at the low ends of the bands that hold 10,296 and 15,840 nodes in 20 of 20 samples: the median gain on the trains of the node types of the sample is 0.10% at 10,241 nodes and -0.47% at 15,361, where all 20 samples lie below zero, and the lowest of the 40 is -0.84%. The tables route Mini at 10,296 and 15,840 atoms to the per-layer kernels, as the alternating replays alone do, and at 15,840 atoms the route of the rules takes 2.2%, 0.1% and 0.5% longer per step on carbon, BCN and five elements. Between two routes neither of which is that of the rules, which the bisection of a band compares, the trains alone decide, by the mean of the two median gains. Where the device idles part of the time, the gain of a route lies between its values in alternating replays and on trains, so a route that the measurement selects is not slower than the rules at a load in between, beyond the tie and the noise of the measurements. On the L20 the tables of this rule keep the native-spin Neo and Air at 100,800 atoms, which the alternating replays alone sent to the per-layer kernels, on the rules, where their LAMMPS step equals that of an archive without a table within 0.1%, and they shorten that step at 8,000 atoms by 7.6% to 8.4%. Against nine maps of the gain under sustained load (`route_probe.py --heat 3 --block`, 201 readings of the ten archives on carbon and BCN), no run size that the tables route away from the rules loses more than 1%, 31 readings lie between a loss of 1% and no gain, and one gain above 1% stays with the rules, that of the trained Air at 12,320 nodes, which gains 1.1% with every node carbon and loses 1.4% with three types. The top of a departure interval lies where the bisection finds the gain on trains crossing the tie, so a sustained reading there can approach a loss of 1%: the readings near the tops of the intervals of the L20 lie lower than those inside them (medians of +0.65% and +1.55%), and the 28 readings at 14 tops have a median of +0.01% and none below -1.00%. A block next to the tie, such as the 32 run sizes of the native-spin Air from 94,209 nodes (a sustained loss of 0.9% to 1.5% in two probes), changes its route from one freeze to the next.

The result, a fitting-route table that names the device it was measured on, is a literal argument of the operator in the frozen program; a table of another device, an empty one, and an archive frozen without one take the routes of the rules. Every route evaluates the same contractions in the same order, so the table changes the speed alone: the LAMMPS dumps of Neo and Air at 16,016 and 17,160 atoms, which two tables route differently, are bitwise identical on carbon and BCN. A run left to the rules repeats their code, device queries included, and not only their route: the occupancy queries of the rules load the module of the fused kernels under lazy module loading even where they then take the per-layer kernels, and on a V100 a table that named the per-layer route for Mini at 512 atoms, and so skipped those queries, ran the identical kernels of the step 2.3% slower on carbon and 2.6% on BCN, by inference through the placement of the modules loaded later.

The measurement adds 21 to 197 s to a freeze on this device, 876 s over the ten archives of the freeze stage of `device_suite.sh`, which takes 1,125 s in all; it takes 42 to 471 s per archive and 1,640 s over the ten on the L20, 1,257 s on the V100 and 1,079 s on the H20. On this device the tables send the width-256 runs of 12,033 to 17,088 nodes of Air and to 18,048 nodes of Neo and of the native-spin Neo and Air to the per-layer kernels, with further bands of the native-spin Air up to 36,704 nodes and of the native-spin Neo up to 48,352, the width-384 runs of four bands between 10,945 and 42,112 nodes to the one-CTA shape and the width-96 runs of 30,081 to 36,096 nodes, and those of the trained Nano from 6,337 to 9,024, to the 64-node panels; the per-layer kernels also take narrow bands below 12,100 nodes of every grade but Nano. Per LAMMPS step, in alternating runs against archives without a table, they shorten Neo at 12,672 atoms by 4.7% and at 17,160 atoms by 1.9% on carbon and 0.2% on BCN, Air at 12,672 and 16,016 atoms by 3.7% and 2.8% on carbon and by 2.8% and 0.7% on BCN, the native-spin Neo and Air at 14,976 atoms by 5.7% to 5.8% and 4.6% to 4.9% on carbon and BCN, and Plus at 896 to 41,040 atoms by 1.3% to 5.8%, while the benchmark sizes keep the routes of the rules. The runs of Air from 17,089 to 18,048 nodes stay with the rules: at 17,160 atoms the per-layer kernels would shorten the step by 2.3% on carbon and lengthen it by 0.4% on BCN. The narrow departures of the tables below 10,000 nodes gain little and lose nothing: the 64-node panels of the trained Nano from 6,337 to 9,024 nodes shorten the step at 7,200 and 8,640 atoms by 0.5% on carbon and BCN, the per-layer kernels of the trained Mini from 3,009 to 3,264 nodes and of the native-spin Mini from 3,873 to 3,968 shorten it at 3,136 and 3,888 atoms by 1.1% and 1.0% on carbon and by 1.5% and 2.1% on BCN, and those of the trained Mini from 1,857 to 1,952 nodes change it at 1,920 atoms by -0.5% on carbon and +0.2% on BCN, within the scatter of their rounds (paired medians of ten alternating rounds on one device, four for the native-spin Mini; the rounds scatter by up to 5% at these sizes).

### 6.7 Register budgets

Every edge kernel is compiled with a minimum resident-block count that sets its register budget. On compute capability 8.9 and 12.x, whose SMs hold at most 24 blocks, the radial-mode kernels of 64 channels and more and the degree-two spin kernels that fit get 80 registers; the rest keep the compiler's own count. The degree-three spin forward kernels spill 56 bytes at 80 registers, and the compiler's count of 96 registers shortens them by 18% to 21% for Air and by 2% to 21% for Plus; the degree-two spin backward below 64 channels spills 136 bytes at 80 registers, and the compiler's count shortens it by 15% to 43% at 8 channels and by up to 14% at 32. The slotted kernels of section 6.4 take one budget on every architecture, the same for four and for eight slots: those that slot the mode profiles keep sixteen resident warps, 128 registers per thread (on 12.0 the slotted forward scan of Air takes 14% less time than at 96 registers), and those that slot the basis functions alone, the kernels of Neo, keep twenty, 96 registers per thread; a co-scheduled block is bounded by its thread count, so the slotted backward of 128 channels, the slotted forward of eight slots of 128 channels and the slotted spin backward of Air in blocks of sixteen rows take 128 registers, and the slotted spin scans of 128 channels in blocks of twenty rows take 96, as do the pairwise spin scans of the profiles that co-schedule, which run blocks of twenty rows where their spin-free counterparts run twenty-four at 80 registers. The budget is a function of the profile, the direction and the architecture, evaluated at compile time.

### 6.8 Export

The AOTInductor program carries source indices as a `uint8[S,W]` tensor, where `W` is dynamic between four and eight bytes. The byte dtype permits one artifact to accept either integer width without copying its weights or index data. The operator validates the supported widths and reinterprets the buffer as typed words; all CSR positions remain int64. The scoped symbolic-shape configuration keeps zero- and one-edge graphs valid through their two-slot guards, during both export and compilation.

Metadata declares `canonical_index_dtype="auto"` and `canonical_index_storage="byte_words"`. Python evaluation chooses source width from the flattened node domain, and native callers declare the width beside their source pointer. Loaders reject fixed-width archives with a re-freeze instruction. The exported ABI has eight tensors for ordinary models and nine for native spin.

The AOTInductor package of a compact canonical lower is compiled with runtime constant folding, so expressions of the weights alone, such as the per-type energy bias that the fused pipeline sums from two parameters, are evaluated once, by the first call after the constants are loaded; an update of the constants for a runtime charge state marks them for evaluation again at the next call, which the replay of section 6.2 runs eagerly, so the folding never runs inside a capture. The fitting head bias is passed to the operator as the parameter itself rather than as a reshaped view, which leaves no constant-producing kernel in the per-step graph. Other lowers are compiled without it: the uncompressed graph lower of a native-spin DPA4 model bridged to ZBL evaluates to NaN under it.

A branch of a multi-task model whose branches share the fitting network carries a one-hot case embedding as the trailing input columns of the first fitting layer, which `fitting_eligible` refuses: such a branch used to export to the graph form with the reference fitting network. The case embedding is a constant of the branch, so the export folds it (`GeneralFitting.fold_case_embedding`, applied by `_fold_case_embeddings` in `serialization.py` to the model that the schema selection deserializes and to the one that is traced, before the vacuum fold): its product with the rows of the first-layer weight that read it is added to the bias, the rows are removed, and the residual flag of the layer is cleared, since the residual was inactive at the original input width and would otherwise become active where the narrower input makes the layer square. The branch then selects the compact canonical schema and runs the fused kernels of a single-task model, at zero cost per step, while `model.json` keeps the branch as trained. A first layer whose residual adds its input, with the descriptor and the case embedding together as wide as the first hidden layer or half as wide, carries the case embedding past the activation and keeps it, which the export reports with a warning; for the presets that happens at 26 branches for Nano, 48 for Mini and Neo and 37 for Air, and never for Plus.

The trained Mini extended by two case columns with zero weights (`debug/cuda_bench/freeze_case_branch.py`), whose branch computes the trained Mini itself, gives LAMMPS dumps bitwise identical to those of the single-task archive on carbon and BCN at 10,296 atoms (`in_dump.lammps`, run 0). Its step takes 0.4448 against 0.4464 ms on carbon and 0.4714 against 0.4698 ms on BCN at 10,296 atoms, while two freezes of the single-task archive differ by 0.2% and 0.75% (medians of six alternating rounds on one device, public LAMMPS), and the route measurement behaves alike on both networks, which differ only in the residual flag of the first layer: three repeats in one process ended in 3.5 to 4.1 s with no departure twice and in 24 s with the band of 3,009 to 3,168 nodes once, on both. The OpenLAM two-branch Mini (OMat24 and OMol25 branches, `dim_case_embd` 8, the vacuum reference and the frame charge state, 28,000 steps into training), frozen per branch with `--head` and `--compress`, takes the compact canonical schema and matches its checkpoint on twelve OMat24 validation frames of 3 to 94 atoms and twelve OMolOpoly frames of 20 to 263 atoms in six charge states to 4.4·10⁻⁷ eV/atom in the energy and 1.6·10⁻⁵ eV/Å in the forces. Its OMat24 branch takes 0.151, 0.449 and 2.905 ms per step on carbon at 512, 10,296 and 100,800 atoms and 0.152, 0.529 and 3.587 ms on BCN, against 1.133, 1.504 and 10.19 ms and 1.174, 1.644 and 11.39 ms as the export wrote it before it folded the case embedding, a factor of 3.1 to 7.7 (medians of two alternating rounds).

## 7. Measurements that shaped the design

### 7.1 Cache-flushed profiles misattribute the edge scans

Nsight Compute by default flushes every cache before each kernel replay and locks the clocks to base. Under those conditions the Neo forward scan at 100,800 atoms took 2.6 ms and a table-free variant 0.67 ms, which pointed at the radial table reads as the dominant cost; in the LAMMPS run the same kernel takes 0.89 ms, because the 0.9 MB table stays resident in L2. The low-rank table, designed on the cache-flushed numbers to cut the table columns by 37%, shortened the real kernel at 100,800 atoms by 1%. Profiling with `--cache-control none --clock-control none` then showed that 38% of the forward's stall samples wait on the bond-vector load that heads every edge and 25% on the table loads, at 24 resident warps per SM: the scans are bound by the latency of that dependent chain, not by table bandwidth. With the bond-vector prefetch of section 6.4 hiding the load from DRAM, the low-rank table gains 3% at 100,800 atoms as well; at 10,296 atoms, where the bond vectors stay in L2, it gives 5% to 11% either way.

Ablations inside LAMMPS at 100,800 atoms, each removing one cost from a scratch build (the outputs are wrong by construction), give the remaining shares of the edge backward: for Nano the source atomics take 26% and the table 19%; for Neo the table takes 15% of the backward and 38% of the forward.

### 7.3 Small and medium systems

At 512 atoms the device is busy between about half and three quarters of the step. On the public LAMMPS the step runs as three consecutive segments (`lmp_nsys.py`, medians over 400 steps; the profiler lengthens every host API call, so the median step of Nano takes 165 µs under it against 136 µs in a plain run):

1. The model graph: 60 µs for Nano, 72 µs for Mini and 185 µs for Plus.
1. LAMMPS, from the end of the graph to the force clear that the pair style launches, about 75 µs under the profiler for every grade: the force scatter of the pair style, the velocity and position updates of `fix nvt` with the kinetic-energy reduction of its temperature (10 µs), the neighbor-distance check of `neigh_modify every 1 check yes` (4 µs) and the forward communication. The host waits for the device twice: for the temperature, from which it integrates the thermostat chain, and for the result of the neighbor check.
1. The host path of the pair style, from the force clear to the first kernel of the graph, about 28 µs under the profiler: the two kernels of the graph build (10 µs of device time together) and the launch of the replay.

The second segment is LAMMPS's own and the same for the baseline on the same executable; the third is the deepmd-kit side, whose graph build in the baseline also reads the edge count back to the host every step. In a plain run the step outside the model graph takes about 76 µs for Nano (136 µs less the 60 µs of the graph), so the 110 µs per step of 5× for Nano (551 µs for the baseline) would leave the model graph about 34 µs, against 60 µs. The largest kernels of the graph at this size are the fused fitting (17 µs), the edge backward (10 µs), the forward scan (8.5 µs) and the node backward (8.2 µs), each a few dependent phases over 512 nodes that occupy part of the device. At 10,296 atoms the device is busy 73% (Nano) to 95% (Plus) of the step (Nsight Systems, public LAMMPS), and the edge scans, the fitting and the graph build dominate with the same latency-bound character as at 100,800 atoms.

### 7.5 Several GPUs

A run on several MPI ranks gives each rank its local atoms and, as nodes of their own, the ghost atoms within the cutoff (the extended node set, section 6.1); the outputs of the ghost nodes are folded onto their owners by the reverse communication of the pair style. LAMMPS keeps its own communication on the device only with an MPI that can send device memory. Otherwise it disables GPU-aware communication: every step it copies the whole position array to the host and back for the forward communication, and the whole force array for the reverse communication of a pair style. With the conda MPICH 5.0.1 of the workstation (no CUDA support), two ranks of Nano at 100,800 atoms on carbon spend 0.7 ms of host time per 2.6 ms step in four synchronous copies of 2.16 MB each (0.4 ms of device time), and the 2-rank step is longer than that of one rank (1.68 ms).

The pair styles therefore fold the ghost outputs themselves wherever LAMMPS would take its host path for the reverse communication of a pair, with MPI that cannot send device memory and under the classic or host settings of the pair communication (`CompactCanonicalGhostFold` in `source/lmp/compact_canonical_graph_kokkos.h`, which reads the same flag as LAMMPS, `reverse_pair_comm_legacy`, named `reverse_pair_comm_classic` before the 10Sep2025 release): they walk the swaps of the Kokkos brick communication in its order, pack the ghost values of each swap on the device, stage only those values through pinned host buffers, and add the received values on the device. An atom receives one addition per swap, as in the host path, so every output equals that of the host path bit for bit: per-atom energy, force and centroid virial at run 0 and the state after 20 NVE steps, on two and three ranks, for Nano and Plus on carbon at 100,800 atoms and on BCN at 10,296, and the same with the magnetic force for four native-spin grades (atom sorting off, which otherwise reorders the summations of LAMMPS run to run). Where LAMMPS uses its device path, the pair styles leave the communication to it. The Kokkos regression of section 3 runs the fold of `dpa4spin/kk` with six and with nine values on two ranks; the fold of `deepmd/kk` rests on the comparisons above alone.

The CUDA-aware runs use the conda-forge Open MPI 5.0.10 with UCX 1.20.1 and a LAMMPS and plugin built against it, with `OMPI_MCA_opal_cuda_support=true` (the package disables its CUDA support in `etc/openmpi-mca-params.conf`) and `UCX_MEMTYPE_CACHE=n`, without which UCX takes a device buffer for host memory and its CMA transport aborts. An 8 MB device-to-device message between GPUs 0 and 1 then takes 397 us. The CUDA-aware dumps equal those of the MPICH fold bit for bit. The support is enabled for launches of several ranks only (`mpirun --mca opal_cuda_support 1 -x UCX_MEMTYPE_CACHE=n`, which `lmp_scan.py` passes for more than one rank): it adds a memory-type query to every MPI call (`cuMemRetainAllocationHandle`, five per step under Nsight Systems), which lengthens the single-rank NVT step by 22 to 30 us (Nano 0.141 → 0.163 ms at 512 atoms and 0.275 → 0.303 ms at 10,296, Neo 0.171 → 0.201 ms at 512; `ab_points.py`, four alternating runs each), and LAMMPS checks for GPU-aware MPI only on several ranks, so a single rank without it runs without a warning. The cost depends on the node: on the H20 node the single-rank medians with and without the support differ by at most 0.4% (`devices/h20.md`, section 9).

A process of Open MPI 5.0 started without `mpirun`, a singleton, counts itself as a local peer, opens the `sm` transport and leaves, when it exits, the segment file of that transport, `/dev/shm/sm_segment.<host>.<uid>.<job>.<rank>`, 16 MiB of which 4 to 140 KB are written, and its session directory, `ompi.<host>.<uid>/jf.0/<job>` in the temporary directory; under `mpirun` the cleanup of the launcher removes both, and the `sm` transport of a singleton is never used (open-mpi/ompi#13261, fixed on the main and v6.0.x branches). Twelve single-rank LAMMPS runs started directly on this workstation left twelve such files and twelve under `mpirun -np 1` none; the Open MPI 4.1.2 of the V100 and the L20 leaves none. Open MPI 5.0.11 (August 2026) does not carry the fix, and 6.0.0 is unreleased (tags `v6.0.0rc1` and `v6.0.0rc2` as of 20 October 2026), so single-rank runs are started directly all the same, as the install page describes: a run leaves 4 to 140 KB of memory in `/dev/shm` and an empty directory, which the removal of the files of ended jobs clears, and a reboot clears `/dev/shm`. A start through `mpirun -np 1` leaves neither but binds its process to core 0, so that concurrent runs on a node share one core, unless `--bind-to none` is given. The placement of the host process also moves the step. On GPU 0 of this workstation, whose NUMA node holds the CPUs 0 to 23 and 96 to 119 of the 192, a process bound to core 0 by `mpirun -np 1` runs the step of Mini at 10,296 atoms 0.9% faster and that of Nano at 512 atoms 2.1% faster than one started directly, which the scheduler places on any CPU (paired medians of six alternating rounds, faster in 6 and in 4 of them; `ab_points.py` with a wrapper for each placement as `BENCH_LMP`). A direct start confined to the CPUs of the NUMA node of the GPU runs Mini at 10,296 atoms as fast as the bound one (+0.2%) and one confined to another node 0.65% slower, so there the locality of the host process to its GPU accounts for the difference, while at 512 atoms, where the step is bound by the host, the binding to one core gains another 2.5%; excluding the `sm` transport from a direct start changes neither. The harness starts single-rank runs directly and unbound, so every step time of this document includes that placement, on both sides of each comparison alike. A run bound to cores of the NUMA node of its GPU would be faster by these margins.

At three ranks of one million atoms the edge scans and the graph build take 0.35 of their one-rank time, the share of the local atoms (333,333 of 1,000,000), while the fitting network, the node backward and the readout take 0.47 to 0.48, the share of local and ghost nodes together (475,760 of 1,000,000): the node-level kernels evaluate the ghost nodes as well, whose energies the ownership mask then discards. In the profiled runs (one run each, under the profiler) that is about 0.5 ms of the 7.2 ms Nano step and 6 to 7 ms of the 43.9 ms Plus step on rank 0, depending on whether the share of the local nodes is taken from the edge scans (0.35) or from the atom count (one third) (section 10).

## 8. Native spin

The native-spin variant compiles the five spin families into the same kernels and closes the magnetic force inside the fused operator: the node kernel forms the on-site part, and the edge backward adds the neighbour part of every edge onto its source node in 64-bit fixed point, like the force (section 6.3). Its edge kernels carry the spin payload in registers, which is why their register budgets differ from the geometric ones (section 6.7) and why their co-scheduled blocks hold twenty rows rather than twenty-four. They run unshared rows in blocks of four independent rows: every block holds the shared memory that the driver reserves per block besides its own, and in one-row blocks the twenty-four resident blocks of the degree-two spin backward of Neo need 34 KB, which exceeds the configuration of 32 KB of shared memory, so the driver takes 64 KB and leaves 64 KB of L1 instead of 96. Four-row blocks keep the 32 KB configuration and shorten the spin step of Neo at 100,800 atoms by 9% on carbon and 21% on BCN (1% at 10,296 atoms), and that of Nano and Mini by up to 4%; the rows stay independent, and the outputs are bit for bit unchanged. Two factors set the Newtonian spin step, and each is measured with the other held fixed.

### 8.2 Integrator

## 10. Remaining directions

The directions below are on the deepmd-kit side, except the last, which concerns the LAMMPS fork of the native-spin path; for the plain models, the cost of LAMMPS itself (section 7.3) is outside them. Optimizations that engage only in systems of one element are not pursued: production systems hold several elements, and the carbon benchmark exercises single-type scans that such systems never take.

- On the frozen geometry the BCN step remains longer than the carbon step at 100,800 atoms by 6% for Neo, 16% for Air and 12% for Plus, and the five-element step by 20%, 38% and 28% (section 7.2): the slotted scans keep the FiLM of the channels below `C1` on the edges, add every basis function to every slot with a weight of zero for the slots that an edge does not belong to, and hold their slot sums at sixteen or twenty resident warps, where the single-type scans run at twenty-four. Mini, which carries neither radial modes nor a low-rank table, keeps the FiLM of its 32 channels on every edge (9% longer on BCN, 39% on five elements); slotting them would hold four copies of every moment of a lane, 56 accumulators, and eight copies for five to eight types.
- Every kernel of a profile with slotted scans is launched for every system, and those that do not serve the system retire their blocks: a grid of one-row blocks takes about 57 µs to retire at 100,800 atoms, a grid of four-row blocks a quarter of that, and the kernels of eight slots add about 1 µs of device time per kernel to the step at 512 atoms (Nsight Systems). On carbon, which no slotted kernel serves, the step at 512 and 10,296 atoms stays within 1.5% of that of the build without slotted kernels (six alternating pairs on the public LAMMPS: Neo 1.5% and 1.1% longer, Nano, Air and Plus within ±1.4%), and the kernels of eight slots leave the steps of carbon and BCN at 10,296 and 100,800 atoms within 1% of those of the build without them. A type count that LAMMPS passes with the graph, or a conditional node of the replayed CUDA graph that the prologue sets, would launch the one kernel.
- A system of more than eight node types scans pairwise, and so does the edge backward of Neo for five to eight types (section 7.4). On one frozen geometry, the sites of the diamond benchmark evaluated without time integration (`in_frozen.lammps`, with the systems of the `energy`, `bcn` and `five` flavors, the last of which places B, C, N, O and F at random), the step of the five-element system at 100,800 atoms takes 1.65 ms for Nano, as with three elements; for the grades that slot, the baseline → the build with slots of four types alone → the current build, Neo 11.51 → 5.29 → 5.21 ms, Air 17.79 → 10.29 → 8.30 ms and Plus 35.96 → 21.26 → 13.39 ms (Nsight Systems, medians of two alternating runs, `work_nsys/t27*`), against 4.64, 6.98 and 11.75 ms with three elements. Mini, which carries neither radial modes nor a low-rank table, takes 4.03 against 3.15 ms: its pairwise scans read the coefficients of the pair of every edge, whose rows for five types no longer stay in L1 (the L1 hit rate of the Mini forward falls from 89% to 71%); a timing-only build in which every edge reads the coefficients of the pair of its centre with itself takes Mini to 3.1 ms on either system, a hint to keep the coefficient lines in L1 (`ld.global.nc.L1::evict_last`) leaves the step unchanged, and marking the table reads first for eviction instead lengthens the step on every system (section 7.4). Staging the coefficients of the types of a row in shared memory, emulated by timing-only builds that keep the dependent load of the source type and hold the shared memory such a stage takes (1.25 KB per row for five types), shortens the five-element step by 13% at 100,800 atoms and by 5% at 10,296, nearly all of it in the forward scan (−37%): the edge backward, which already holds 1.15 KB per block, then falls into the next shared-memory configuration and keeps 9% of its time. On BCN the stage lengthens the step (+1.4% without the type dependency), and a stage in the forward alone would shorten it by about 2%. A forward kernel of Mini that stages the coefficients would therefore pay off on systems of five or more types, by about 10% at 100,800 atoms. In NVT runs the five-element system on these sites heats to about 4,800 K within 40 steps and rebuilds its neighbor list about every seven steps, so its whole-step timings mix the cost of the types with that of a disordered structure, whose bonds spread over the whole radial table.
- The native-spin forward of Neo at 100,800 atoms reads the 64 columns of its direct table in blocks of independent rows; co-scheduled blocks of twenty-four rows take 1.3 ms against 1.5 to 1.8 ms in a scratch build, which shortens the step by 3% on carbon and 7% on BCN, but lengthen the forward at 10,296 atoms by 7% to 10% (1.5% of the step) and change its summation order.
- A trained native-spin model would read the low-rank table for its channels from `C1` on, which the spin families do not read; the compression builds the table only for spin-free models, and no trained spin checkpoint exists to measure it.
- The neighbour part of the magnetic force is added onto the source of every edge with three 64-bit atomics; a scratch build without them is 11% shorter for the Nano spin step at 100,800 atoms, 6% for Mini and 1% to 3% for the others. Adding the contributions per source in shared memory first keeps the fixed-point sums exact, but needs the edges of a block grouped by source.
- The pair style launches the two kernels of the graph build and the replay of the model graph from the host every step, about 28 µs of the 165 µs step of Nano at 512 atoms under the profiler (section 7.3). Capturing the graph build into the replayed graph, which then needs the addresses of the LAMMPS neighbor list in its key, would leave one launch per step.
- The weight transpose of the fused fitting runs every call (1.4 µs for Nano at 512 atoms, up to 4.5 µs for Plus); a transposed copy of the weights produced at export and passed to the fused operator would remove it.
- The fused fitting at a width of 96 reaches 38 TFLOP/s against 52 TFLOP/s at a width of 256. A warp of the fused kernel arranges its lanes as 4 rows by 8 columns of four channels each, so a width of 96 admits tiles of 4 × 4, 8 × 4 and 4 × 12 or 8 × 12 accumulators per lane but none of 8 × 8, the tile of the wide shape. An arrangement of 8 lane rows by 4 lane columns would give 8 × 8 tiles in CTAs of three warps and keep the canonical order of the energy head, two of its three butterfly levels across lanes and the third within a lane. The shapes measured at this width (section 7.4) favour resident warps over tile size: 8 × 4 tiles on 12 warps per multiprocessor take 0.283 ms at 100,800 nodes against 0.281 ms for 4 × 4 tiles on 24, and 8 × 8 tiles would leave six warps per multiprocessor, so their gain, at most the 0.07 ms between the rates of the narrow and the wide width, is uncertain.
- The edge scans at 10,000 atoms and above are latency bound at 24 resident warps per SM; more independent edges per warp at the same register state is the remaining lever.
- On compute capability 8.9 the co-scheduled edge backward of Air keeps 120 bytes of local memory at the 80 registers that its 768 threads leave, against 72 bytes for the same source compiled for 12.0 by the same nvcc, and the eight-slot backward of Plus 120 bytes against 48 bytes for blocks of one row. On an L20 the co-scheduled blocks lengthen the step of Air on carbon by 3.8% and that of Plus on five elements by 3.7% at 10,296 atoms, while blocks of independent rows lengthen that of Plus on carbon by 4.8% and 13.7% at 10,296 and 100,800 atoms and on BCN by 3.8% and 6.9% (six alternating LAMMPS runs). No threshold on the local memory of the two instances separates the cases that lose from those that win, and the co-scheduled blocks change the summation order, so they stay a function of the system, the kernel and the device (section 9) rather than a choice measured at freeze time; a register state of the co-scheduled scans that fits 80 registers on 8.9 as well would remove the loss.
- On compute capability 7.0, whose kernel images stage the neighbor lists of the single-pass fill through registers in place of `cp.async`, the twelve-candidate fill keeps a 64-byte stack frame at the 64 registers of its bound of four blocks per multiprocessor and takes 174 µs, 18% of the step of Nano, at 10,296 atoms on a V100. A bound of three blocks, 80 registers, would remove the spill at the cost of a quarter of the resident warps; on 12.0, where the kernel does not spill, the four blocks are the faster bound (section 7.4). The bounds are compile-time arguments of `Kokkos::LaunchBounds` in `source/lmp/compact_canonical_graph_kokkos.h`, so a value for 7.0 would follow the Kokkos architecture of the build, and the gain is a few percent of the Nano step at most.
- The graph build at 100,800 atoms moves its 385 MB at 70% of the DRAM bandwidth (section 6.1), and 254 MB of that is the edge storage it writes; 31% of its stall samples fall on the staging of the neighbor list. A fill that overlaps the staging of the next centers with the emit of the current ones needs the state of both at once, which the register budget of four blocks per multiprocessor does not hold (section 7.4); a staging buffer of its own for the next centers would halve the resident blocks instead.
- The rows of the source CSR are sorted by a warp per row in registers when the candidate lists fit 512 entries, and by a Kokkos team sort per row otherwise; the second serves large cutoffs, which take the counted fill as well, and has not been timed.
- The bridged edge backward of Plus keeps 144 bytes of stack against 96 for the plain kernel; a window state that fits the registers of the plain kernel would remove most of the 8.8% the bridge costs at 10,296 atoms.
- On several ranks the fused operator evaluates the descriptor readout, the fitting network and the node backward for the ghost nodes too, whose rows are empty and whose energies the mask discards (section 7.5); skipping them would shorten the three-rank step of one million atoms by about 7% for Nano and 14% to 16% for Plus. The operator knows the number of local nodes only as a device tensor, so the skip needs that count on the host: either a shape the canonical lower carries, such as the ghost types as an input of their own, or a read of the device value outside capture.
- Under several MPI ranks or a fixed boundary, the spin atom style exchanges the spin velocity and inertia through the host at every neighbor-list rebuild; adding them to the Kokkos exchange would keep that path on the device as well.

## 11. The Wisevolve Potential Engine

The Wisevolve Potential Engine (WPE, Bu and Fan, arXiv:2610.17366) is a closed-source GPU potential-evaluation backend for GPUMD, distributed as one proprietary shared library (`libwisevolve_potential.so.1.0.0`, 575 MB) behind an open C interface and an open GPUMD adapter. It reaches a reported geometric-mean speedup of 3.49 over native GPUMD across 106 neuroevolution-potential (NEP) workloads on an RTX 5090, and it reaches that speedup by the same principle this path rests on: within a simulation stage the model and the required outputs are fixed, so a specialized execution policy can be selected once and reused across steps. This section records what the principle costs a generic NEP code and what WPE does with it, because the floor of a generic NEP kernel is the floor this path's design avoids, and because two of WPE's levers are not yet taken here. The study reads the kernel metadata and the disassembly of the installed binary and the open interface (`include/wisevolve_potential_api.h`, `integration/gpumd/`); it recovers no source, and the engine's licence forbids both reverse engineering and redistribution, so the findings inform this path alone and nothing of the engine is carried into it. Its artifacts live on the workstation under `/nas/outisli/Software/WPE/` (`prof/`, `bench/`, `verify/`, `re2/resusage_sm120.txt`, `REVERSE_ENGINEERING_FINDINGS.md`).

### 11.1 Measurement

The comparison builds native GPUMD and WPE-enabled GPUMD from one source tree (GPUMD master `98f22dba`, `make` and `make -f makefile_wpe` with `-std=c++17 -arch=sm_120` under CUDA 13.3) and runs them on the RTX PRO 6000 of section 2.1, one card per run. The systems are the NEP cases of the engine's own benchmark set (`Wisevolve-Potential-Engine/examples/benchmarks/nep/cases`), each replicated to roughly 0.4 to 0.5 million atoms and run for 100 warm-up and 500 timed steps under the Nosé-Hoover-chain NVT and the stochastic-cell-rescaling NPT ensembles of GPUMD. The gain is entirely in the potential evaluation. On diamond Si with NEP89 at 393,984 atoms (602 force evaluations), the total device kernel time of the step falls from 5,068 ms to 1,665 ms, a factor of 3.04 (Nsight Systems). Native GPUMD spends that time in a few heavy kernels -- `find_descriptor` 53.7% (4,519 µs per call), `find_partial_force_angular` 22.3%, `find_force_radial` 7.5%, `gpu_find_force_many_body` 6.9%, the neighbor build 5.6% and ZBL 1.6% -- while WPE spreads a third of the time over more, lighter kernels: the angular force scan 24.8%, the angular descriptor 20.7%, the fitting-network evaluation 18.0%, the radial descriptor 13.3%, the radial force scan 10.4% and ZBL 3.0%. The two implementations agree numerically to single-precision roundoff: a 20-step NVE run of a 13,824-atom Si cell differs by at most 2.49·10⁻⁴ eV in the total energy (1.8·10⁻⁸ eV/atom) and 8·10⁻⁷ K in the temperature, the difference of two summation orders of the same fp32 arithmetic, so the speed is not bought with precision.

### 11.2 The specialized-kernel library

The engine ships one shared library whose fatbinary holds 27 kernel families for each of eight architectures (compute capability 7.0, 7.5, 8.0, 8.6, 8.9, 9.0, 10.0 and 12.0) and links no runtime compiler (`cuobjdump --list-elf`; no NVRTC or nvJitLink among its needed libraries). The kernels are therefore compiled ahead of time and selected, not generated, at run time. The selection runs at the stage boundary through the open C interface: `wpe_create` reads the potential file and returns the cutoff and an opaque context bound to the model; `wpe_activate_stage` receives a `WpeStageInfo` that carries the stage requirements as a bitmask of fact bits (which consumers read the system or per-atom virial, whether the run is HNEMD, whether it needs charge or Born-charge output, the PIMD bead count, the qNEP reciprocal-space method and the DFT-D3 settings) and selects the execution policy; `wpe_bind` takes the GPUMD-owned device pointers and the box; and `wpe_compute` is the hot path, with the binding reused across steps while it is unchanged. The adapter collects the fact bits by observing the `run.in` command stream (`wpe_stage.cu`), so a run that never dumps a virial, for instance, activates a stage without the virial-consumer bit.

The kernel names encode the four selection axes the interface exposes, so the policy is legible in the symbol table. For the NEP89 model above -- `n_max 4 4`, `basis_size 8 8`, `l_max 4 2 1`, hidden width 80 -- the descriptor is built by `build_descriptor_r5x9_angular_all5` and `build_descriptor_r5x9_filter_radial`, where `r5x9` is the radial shape `(n_max_radial + 1) × (basis_size_radial + 1) = 5 × 9` and `all5` the full set of three-, four- and five-body angular terms; the network runs as `evaluate_ann_unary_fused_fb<35>`, whose template argument is the descriptor dimension (five radial plus `(n_max_angular + 1) × (l_max_3body + 1 + 1) = 5 × 6 = 30` angular); and the forces run as `find_force_angular_l4_none_static_q_single_type` and `find_force_radial_specialized_none_sorted_global_owner_suffix`. The suffixes name the stage and structure axes: `static_q` fixes the descriptor dimension at compile time against a `runtime_q` fallback, `none` selects the variant that writes no virial against `total` and `peratom`, `single_type` specializes the one-element system against the pairwise and slotted variants, `batch` fuses the beads of a PIMD stage into one launch, and `small_box` handles the periodic images of a small cell. The library carries the whole menu -- radial shapes `r5x9`, `r10x10` and `r5x13` beside a `generic` fallback, the three virial variants, `static` and `runtime` descriptor dimensions, single-type, slotted and pairwise scans, batched and small-box forms, and several network strategies (`unary` for NEP against `grouped` for qNEP, a `subwarp8` form that assigns eight lanes to an atom's network, and `matrix` forms) -- and `wpe_activate_stage` picks the leaf that matches the model shape, the stage requirements and the execution structure of the run.

### 11.4 Where the generic kernels lose their time

The counters name one cause, and the mechanical experiment of section 11.5 shows that the rest are its consequences.

**The spill.** The native `find_descriptor` keeps a 920-byte stack frame, and its disassembly reaches the 64 KB display cap with 409 loads and 463 stores to local memory already emitted; `find_partial_force_angular` keeps a 5,984-byte frame and emits 73 and 59 before the same cap. Executed, the descriptor writes and reads 881 million 32-byte sectors of local memory in one call at 393,984 atoms, 28 GB, and the angular force kernel another 423 million, 13.5 GB, against 0.2 GB for every kernel of WPE together. The stalls follow: the native descriptor spends 14.65 cycles per issued instruction waiting on a scoreboard dependency of an L1TEX access, 70.8% of its 20.8-cycle warp cycle, and issues one instruction per 5.3 cycles; WPE's radial descriptor spends 5.8 cycles, its network 1.3. The traffic is not a bandwidth problem -- 28 GB in 4.5 ms is 6.2 TB/s, well inside the L1 aggregate -- it is a latency problem, because the spilled values sit on the dependency chain of the inner loops.

**Why the spill exists.** The arrays are sized for the worst supported model and indexed by run-time values: `float q[MAX_DIM]` and `float Fp[MAX_DIM]` with `MAX_DIM = 103`, `float fn12[MAX_NUM_N]` and `float fnp12[MAX_NUM_N]` with `MAX_NUM_N = 17`, and `float sum_fxyz[NUM_OF_ABC * MAX_NUM_N]` with `NUM_OF_ABC = 80`, which is 80 × 17 floats, 5,440 bytes, in the angular force kernel alone. A loop bound read from `paramb` (`for (n = 0; n <= paramb.n_max_radial; ++n)`) makes every access to those arrays a dynamic index, and a dynamic index into a local array is a local-memory access by construction whatever the size of the array. The same run-time bounds also forbid the transformations that would remove work: the generic angular descriptor puts the channel loop outside the neighbour loop, five traversals of the same row with the geometry recomputed each time, and with `n_max_angular` a run-time value the compiler can neither unroll the outer loop nor hoist the geometry out of it.

**What the spill is not.** The five-pass structure, the fusion of the descriptor and the network into one kernel, and the run-time `L_max` switch of `accumulate_s` are real differences from WPE, and the experiment of section 11.5 removes none of them: it keeps the five passes, keeps the fusion and keeps the switch, and still matches WPE's descriptor phase. Once the bounds are template arguments the compiler unrolls the outer loop, hoists the geometry and fuses the passes itself, and the resulting kernel is 1,696 instructions with 28 `LDL` and 81 `STL` against 4,096 or more with 409 and 463. The source-level restructuring WPE performs is what a compiler does on its own when it is allowed to know the shape.

Two secondary effects remain visible but do not drive the gap. The generic build chooses the register count of the fused kernel for the worst of its three phases: 127 registers hold the descriptor and the network at 32% occupancy, where WPE gives each phase its own budget. And the generic force kernels always accumulate the nine components of the per-atom virial, which `none` variants drop; the native build's `gpu_find_force_many_body` pays a different price for the same design, reading the 120 stored angular moments of every atom back from global memory at 8.8% issue and 81 cycles of long-scoreboard stall per instruction.

### 11.5 The mechanical specialization

To measure the mechanism rather than infer it, the two heaviest generic kernels were copied and specialised with no change of arithmetic: the shape became template arguments (`NR`, `BR`, `NA`, `BA`, `LM`, the descriptor dimension, the type count and the `has_q_*` flags), every `paramb` field that carries the shape was replaced by its constant, and the two arrays of the angular force kernel were sized to the shape (`sum_fxyz` 1,360 to 120 floats, `Fp` 90 to 30) with the matching `accumulate_f12<24>` instantiation. The dispatcher selects the specialised copy when the model is the NEP89 shape and the generic one otherwise. Nothing else changed: the five-pass angular loop, the fused network and the `L_max` switch are all still there.

| Kernel                            | Native (µs) | Specialised (µs) | WPE (µs) |
| --------------------------------- | ----------: | ---------------: | -------: |
| Descriptor and network            |       4,251 |            1,377 |    1,438 |
| Angular force                     |       1,818 |            1,198 |      729 |
| Radial force                      |         609 |              609 |      288 |
| Many-body force                   |         559 |              559 |          |
| Whole step (10⁷ atom·step/s, ±5%) |        4.78 |             8.48 |    14.50 |

The stack frames fall from 920 to 440 bytes and from 5,984 to 640, the static instruction counts from 4,096 or more, the display cap in both cases, to 1,696 and 1,656, and the local-memory instructions from at least 409 and 463, and 73 and 59, to 28 and 81, and 18 and 42. The outputs are unchanged: a 20-step NVE run of the 13,824-atom cell agrees with the native binary in the total energy to the last printed digit at every step, and the energy is a function of the forces of every step before it, so the forces are identical too.

Two conclusions follow. The descriptor phase of WPE is fully explained by the compile-time shape: the specialised copy is 3.09x faster than the native kernel against WPE's 2.96x, and it reaches that with the descriptor, the network and the five angular passes still fused into one kernel as the generic code has them: no fission, no `all5` rewrite and no lane split. The angular force is half explained: 1.52x against 2.49x, and the remainder is the design difference the specialisation does not touch, the stored `sum_fxyz` round trip that WPE replaces with recomputation and the eight-lane split of WPE against the thread-per-atom of the generic kernel. The radial force and the many-body force were left unpatched and stay at 609 and 559 µs against WPE's 288 and nothing.

The experiment also gives the negative result that motivates doing it this way. A build that only trims the capacities, `MAX_NUM_N` 17 to 9, `MAX_DIM` 103 to 40 and `MAX_DIM_ANGULAR` 90 to 32, cuts the frames to 560 and 3,120 bytes and buys 1.5% and 3.3% on the two kernels. Capacity without constant bounds is worth nothing; the bound is the lever. For completeness, GPUMD's own `src/main_nep/nep_specialized.cu` applies the same technique to its training kernels through a run-time `nvcc` and a generated `nep_special_config.cuh`, so the principle is already accepted in this code base; WPE's contribution is the delivery of it to the MD path as an ahead-of-time library, which the absence of NVRTC among its dependencies confirms.

### 11.6 Reconstruction of the hot kernels

The five kernels below are reconstructed from their symbol, their launch geometry, the resource table of the fatbinary, the control flow and the operation mix of the SASS, and the open-source NEP equations they evaluate; where a detail is inferred rather than read it is marked. All five run one thread per atom unless stated.

**`build_descriptor_r5x9_filter_radial`** -- 592 instructions, 56 registers, a 32-byte frame, 3 `LDL` and 2 `STL`, one thread per atom, 64-thread blocks, 64.5% occupancy. Per atom it walks the radial neighbour list once, computes the displacement from the packed atom record, the cutoff and its derivative, and the nine basis functions, and contracts them with the 5 × 9 filter block of the type pair into five channel sums, which it stores. It is the generic radial loop with the loop bounds, the type-pair index and the channel count as compile-time constants.

**`build_descriptor_r5x9_angular_all5<64>`** -- 3,832 instructions, 167 registers, a 96-byte frame, 42 `LDL` and 52 `STL`, one thread per atom, 31 KB of dynamic shared memory per block, 12.2% occupancy. Its inner loop is the 626-instruction body described in section 11.4: one angular neighbour per iteration, the displacement and the nine basis functions computed once, and the 24 components of each of the five channels accumulated in registers, which is why the kernel needs 167 of them. The 31 KB of shared memory is 480 bytes per atom of the block, the size of the five 24-component sums; the kernel writes it only before and after the neighbour loop, never inside it, so it is a staging buffer for the block, not the accumulator (inferred: the block-level transform that turns the per-atom sums into coalesced global stores). Section 11.5 shows that a compiler given the constant bounds performs the same transformation, which is why the specialised copy of the generic kernel, five passes and all, matches the phase this kernel serves.

**`evaluate_ann_unary_fused_fb<35>`** -- 912 instructions, 40 registers, no frame, no local access, 128-thread blocks and four threads per atom, 5.7 KB of shared memory, 94% occupancy. Four lanes share one atom: each owns a slice of the 80 hidden units, reads the 35 descriptor values of the atom from shared memory, evaluates its slice and its derivative, and the lanes reduce the energy between them with warp shuffles -- the 24 `SHFL` of the kernel. It computes the forward value and the 35 input gradients in one pass and writes both, which is what the `fused_fb` of its name means; the generic build computes the same pair inside the descriptor kernel with the descriptor still in registers.

**`find_force_radial_specialized_none_sorted_global_owner_suffix<4, 8, true>`** -- 856 instructions, 64 registers, a 32-byte frame, 98,496 blocks of 128 threads, which is 32 launched threads per atom, 60.5% occupancy, no virial. Per edge it computes the cutoff and its derivative and the nine basis functions and their derivatives, contracts them with the filter block of the type pair and with the descriptor gradient of both endpoints, and accumulates the force of the edge on both atoms: the `owner_suffix` of the name is the second direction, added by the same thread that owns the first, so that no atomic is needed and the summation order is fixed (inferred from the name, the absence of atomics in the SASS, and the fixed-point design of the reference path).

**`find_force_angular_l4_none_static_q_single_type<5, 9, 48>`** -- 1,792 instructions, 128 registers, 3.7 KB of static shared memory, 98,496 blocks of 32 threads, eight threads per atom, 32.5% occupancy, no virial. Per edge it recomputes the nine basis functions and the 24 angular moments from the neighbour geometry rather than reading them back from global memory, contracts them with the 30 descriptor gradient components through the `L = 4` moment recursion unrolled at compile time, and produces the three force components of the edge. Its fused multiply-add share of the instruction mix, 38% of the pipe, is the same as that of the generic kernel: this kernel does not do less arithmetic per edge, it does less memory and fewer index operations, and it does them in 1,280 issue slots per atom against 3,767.

### 11.7 Relation to this path

This path already rests on the principle the study isolates, and in a stronger form. The compact canonical schema specializes the compiled program to the model, the fused fitting network of section 6.6 selects its shape by width at freeze time and compiles each width ahead of time, the single-type and slotted edge scans of section 6.4 specialize by the node types of the system, the register budgets of section 6.7 are compile-time functions of the architecture, and the export folds the fixed inputs of a deployed model into the weights -- the vacuum reference and the case embedding of a shared-fitting branch (section 6.8) -- as WPE folds the facts of a stage into its kernel choice. The fused energy-force operator of section 6.3 prunes the per-atom virial that a caller declines, which is WPE's `none` axis, and accumulates the force in reproducible fixed point where WPE fixes a summation order.

What the comparison does not offer is a kernel to copy. The descriptor and force kernels of this path already carry compile-time shapes, no local memory, and one pass over the edges; the angular scan assigns a destination row to a warp and the channels to lane groups, which is the same fission WPE reaches with `all5` and with eight threads per atom. The floor the study locates in the generic NEP kernel, the spill of a run-time-bounded descriptor loop, is the floor the compiled kernels here are built to avoid, and the `cuobjdump -res-usage` check of section 3 guards against it.

Two levers do remain, and both are outside the kernels.

The first is the neighbour subsystem. WPE spends 119 µs of a step on it and rebuilds its spatial structure only when a displacement check fires: `nep_fast_gather_check_clear` costs 25.6 µs per step and the rebuilds amortize over about forty steps. The native build spends 477 µs of a step, 441 of them rebuilding its cell list every step. This path spends 0.31 ms of the 1.67 ms step at 100,800 atoms building the compact graph, 18% of the step, which is 3.1 ns per atom against 1.2 ns for the native cell list; the graph carries the model input this path needs rather than a cell list, so the comparison sets the scale of the phase and not a gap of the same kind. A build that detects an unchanged LAMMPS neighbour list could keep the topology and rewrite only the bond vectors, 12 of the 16 bytes per edge; the saving is bounded by the 0.22 ms floor of the fill and is likely a third of it, 4% to 6% of the Nano step, and it needs the neighbour rebuild counter of LAMMPS as an input to the replay key.

The second is the narrow-network phase of the small grades. WPE's network kernel reaches 94% occupancy by giving an atom to four lanes; the fused operator of this path evaluates the fitting network as one node panel of the fused pipeline, and whether Nano and Mini, whose networks are 96 and 192 wide, would gain from the same split is a measurement of the res-usage and occupancy of the network section of the fused operator, not a design question. The directions of section 10 already carry the related item, and the study moves it up: the two shapes of this path that the measurement of section 7.4 left below 40 TFLOP/s are exactly the narrow ones.

### 12.3 What was tried and did not help

Two changes that target the counters of section 12.2 were built and measured against the same point, and neither moved it.

The first marks the bond-vector loads of the scans as streaming (`__ldcs`), so that the 191 MB of edge vectors stop evicting the radial table from L1: 59,657 against 59,781 atoms/ms at 100,800 atoms, within the spread of the point. The table of the eight-channel grade is 38 KB and already resident; the misses are the streaming reads themselves and not a victim of them.

The second spends registers to buy warps. The narrow scans compile to 80 registers, which caps a multiprocessor at twenty-four one-warp blocks; a launch bound of thirty-two blocks forces 64 registers and eight more resident warps at the cost of a 256-byte spill. The step is unchanged, 1.673 against 1.672 ms: the extra warps hide exactly the latency that the spill adds, so the occupancy of the scan is at a local optimum in both directions.

An explicit-`fmaf` rewrite of the harmonic derivatives was also evaluated under a shared explicit-contraction contract. It passes the layout and reference-model checks, but its performance does not justify inclusion. On H20, ordinary Nano at 100,800 atoms improves by about 0.38% on carbon and BCN, while Nano native spin on BCN slows from 5.03775 to 5.07586 ms and Neo native spin with ZBL slows from 14.0909 to 14.8425 ms, each a four-repetition median against the unfused derivative with the same fixed layout. The Neo regression occurs despite a reduction from 128 to 96 registers in the relevant kernel. Mini on RTX PRO 6000 has overlapping timing ranges. Plus native spin on H20 improves from 37.24770 to 37.18595 ms, about 0.166%, with separated sample ranges; this small gain does not offset the Neo regression. The rewrite is excluded; kernel resource counts alone do not predict whole-step time.

An explicit-contraction implementation couples both geometries to the vector kernel's row partition and compiles DPA4C with `--fmad=false`, retaining contraction only at explicit `fmaf` calls. It passes exact layout comparisons and reference-model accuracy checks, but the complete two-device matrix does not support it as a general optimization. In the eight-point automatic-geometry gate, Nano energy on BCN at 100,800 atoms costs 9.25% on RTX PRO 6000 and 5.76% on H20 against the matched fixed-32 implementation. Profiling attributes the largest vector-path increase to graph filling. A private 16-byte native producer record reduces the fixed-vector width cost to 3.40% and 2.29%, and retaining its native signed-32 source field gives 2.98% and 1.70%. These measurements retain public 64-bit indices; the indexed path still pays for wider records in the model. The automatic-width design keeps the compact native representation where the actual index domain permits it and retains int64 edge positions independently.

A separate 64-bit indexed-decoder experiment loads three low coordinate words and a full-width source instead of two aligned wide loads. It preserves the wrapped coordinates and model-node indices above 2^40 and 2^62, passes the focused layout and memory checks, and nevertheless lengthens the Nano indexed step on BCN at 100,800 atoms by 2.41% on RTX PRO 6000 and 3.53% on H20. Four scalar loads still touch the same memory sectors and add instructions; nominally requesting fewer coordinate bytes does not establish lower DRAM traffic. The aligned-load control remains in the experiment archive; production contains no indexed decoder.

The normal-contraction automatic-width indexed implementation also fails the whole-step acceptance criterion. Across 120 configurations per device, its net time reduction against the retained vector implementation ranges from -14.159% to +12.181% on RTX PRO 6000 (median -0.126%), and from -0.783% to +6.011% on H20 (median -0.016%). The incremental width cost against the matched fixed-32 indexed implementation stays below 3% in that matrix, but that does not establish a net benefit over the original vector path. Several workstation cases regress, and the layout selector adds calibration, extra allocations and a second capture pool. The final implementation removes indexed geometry, its selector and both-layout ABI entirely, while retaining automatic source width and int64 edge positions. The complete [rejected study](../../debug/lmp_perf/graph_geometry/results/rejected_indexed_geometry.md) and raw per-device records retain the original correctness, timing and memory evidence. Subsequent diagnostics stopped at the user's closure request; incomplete diagnostics are not acceptance evidence.

### 12.4 The floor

The step executes 929 million warp instructions at 100,800 atoms (the two scans 537 M, the fitting 295 M, the readout and node backward about 80 M, the prologue and epilogue the rest). A multiprocessor of this part issues four warp instructions per cycle, so the instruction stream alone bounds the step at 0.65 ms even if every scheduler issued every cycle, which no memory-bound kernel does. The graph build adds a floor of its own: 385 MB at the 90th percentile of the DRAM bandwidth is 0.24 ms. For this vector representation, those terms sum to about 0.90 ms, a factor of 1.83, while 2× would need 0.836 ms.

The realistic ceiling is lower than that bound. The scans run at 36% to 39% of issue on the narrow grades because the dependent chain of an edge is longer than the arithmetic it feeds, and no change of occupancy, caching hint or register budget moved it; the fitting is at 71% of issue and 53% of the FMA pipe; the graph build is at 68% of the DRAM bandwidth. Pushing each to a plausible 70% to 80% of its ceiling shortens the step by 5% to 15%, not by 50%, and the small systems cannot gain more than the third of their step that is not device time, most of which belongs to LAMMPS.

No measured experiment produces another doubling. A larger gain still needs evidence for reducing the instruction stream or its latency. Two further candidates are a lower-rank expansion of the angular moments of the narrow grades, which is the lever section 6.4 uses for the wide ones and which an eight-channel descriptor cannot use, and a cheaper representation of the descriptor itself, which changes the model and needs retraining or an error bound.
